Welcome,
Gotham Tech.

This spring, we'll work together to build a school-owned DEEDS instructional model grounded in the needs, strengths, realities, and aspirations of your classrooms, your teachers, and your students. Every step of this process is designed with and for your team. This hub is your home base for the journey.

From Our Team

A Quick Hello

Before we dig in, we wanted to say hi and give you a sense of who we are, what this partnership is about, and why we're excited to do this work with you.

How We Work

What to Expect From Us

This isn't a consulting engagement where we hand you a binder and wish you luck.

🤝

We Build With You

Your teachers and leaders shape every decision. We bring frameworks, facilitation, and broad experience implementing DEEDS in a range of settings. You bring the context and deep expertise on your school and community context.

🔍

Evidence First

No guessing. Every design choice starts with what we actually see and hear in classrooms, surveys, and conversations, combined with what research tells us about the science of learning and development.

📝

Drafts Before Finals

You'll see our work early and often. We build in feedback loops at every stage so that there are no surprises.

🏆

It's Yours

Everything we create is meant to help the Gotham Tech team be sustainable over time — building internal capacity, shared expectations, and systems that strengthen the work year over year.

Phase Two

Build the Instructional Playbook

This phase translates the insights from the Current State Blueprint into a shared instructional model for Gotham Tech. Together we will define what strong challenge-based learning looks like and create a playbook that helps teachers consistently design and implement rigorous, real-world learning experiences.

April – May 2026

Playbook and Scope & Sequence work are running in parallel this spring to give each track more time and depth. Design Team sessions are scheduled on a rolling basis, with weekly leadership check-ins every Friday at 12:30. Between meetings, Transcend drafts and refines materials.

Who Does What

Our Roles in This Phase

Here's how we split the work so nothing falls through the cracks.

T

  • Build instructional protocols, planning templates, and classroom look-fors
  • Develop scope & sequence in parallel with playbook work

Gotham Tech Will
  • Participate in Design Team sessions as scheduled
  • Attend weekly Friday leadership check-ins at 12:30
  • Make decisions with leadership about who will pilot DEEDS modules in the fall
  • Give honest, specific feedback on draft playbook components
  • Final leadership sign-off confirming alignment with school priorities

Now we take the playbook off the page and map it to the year — building challenge-based curriculum overviews grounded in standards and teacher input.

April – May 2026 · Parallel Track
Who Does What

Our Roles in This Phase

T
Transcend Will
  • Facilitate standards mapping and challenge ideation sessions
  • Co-develop scope & sequence and learning journeys alongside Design Team teachers
  • Incorporate feedback from students, partners, and leadership and support finalization of curriculum materials
GT
Gotham Tech Will
  • Share standards and current S&Ss for pilot courses
  • Co-develop challenge concepts, scope & sequence, and learning journeys alongside Transcend
  • Identify community and industry partners to match challenge ideas
  • Gather and share feedback from students and leadership before finalization
How This Works

The Process

First: A Key Decision
Week of April 27

Pilot Scope

Before S&S work can begin, the Design Team aligns on scope: how many full DEEDS units and DEEDS-lite units will each Design Team teacher pilot, and which sections are included. Leadership confirms once the Design Team has aligned.

1
Teachers Share Standards and Current S&Ss
Week of Apr 27 Juhrend, Lynch, Ayoub
Design Team teachers share standards and current scope and sequences for the courses selected in the pilot scope decision. Sarah reviews these ahead of Session 2.
Sarah's working knowledge of the standards terrain for each course
2
Standards Mapping, Challenge Ideation, and Early Feedback
Session 2 · Week of May 5 Sarah (facilitator) Juhrend, Lynch, Ayoub Natalie Leadership Juan & Bradley
The core working session. In breakouts, teachers use Sarah's templates to map their standards to challenge contexts and generate draft challenge ideas. Natalie participates to ground ideation in community context. Juan, Bradley, and leadership are present to react to challenge ideas in real time — getting early feedback while there's still room to iterate in the moment.
Draft challenge ideas per course, shaped by immediate student and leadership input
3
Natalie Identifies Potential Partners
Week of May 5–9 Natalie
Following the ideation session, Natalie works to match draft challenge ideas with potential community and industry partners — what's available, what partners can offer, and any constraints that should shape challenge design.
Partner match options per challenge concept
Note on iteration: Steps 4–6 run in two loose loops rather than a straight line. A first pass produces draft challenges and initial learning journey sketches. A second pass refines both based on partner input and any remaining feedback before finalization. Expect some back-and-forth between challenge framing and journey development.
4
Draft Learning Journeys
Weeks of May 12–16 Sarah Juhrend, Lynch, Ayoub
With draft challenges stable enough, Sarah works with teachers to sketch the learning journeys — how students move through each DEEDS unit, what they do in each phase, and how competencies are demonstrated. These are drafts, not finals; they'll be refined after leadership review.
Draft learning journeys per unit
5
Leadership Review
May 23 · Friday Check-In Luis, Kimberly, Margaret
Leadership reviews the draft S&S and learning journey sketches together before finalization — challenge scope, partner alignment, and unit structure. This gate prevents late-stage surprises and gives leadership the chance to flag anything that conflicts with scheduling or pathway expectations.
Sign-off or targeted revision asks
6
Revise and Finalize S&S and Learning Journeys
May 27–30 Sarah Juhrend, Lynch, Ayoub
Final pass incorporating feedback from Natalie, students, and leadership. S&S and learning journeys are revised and locked together, ready for fall implementation.
Final S&S and learning journey documents per unit — ready for fall

    Transcend Framework
    Conditions for Innovation
    Conviction
    A deep and sustaining belief in the importance and potential of the work being undertaken that fuels engagement and ensures it is prioritized. Includes conviction in the potential of all young people, in a vision of extraordinary learning, to engage in a community-based process, and through prioritization.
    Clarity
    A comprehensive and crisp understanding of the work ahead that provides direction and a path forward. Includes clarity in the current state, in vision, and in the journey path.
    Capacity
    The support of personnel and time required to successfully carry out the work. Includes capacity in skills and capabilities, in time and bandwidth, and through continuity of people in key roles.
    Coalition
    The investment of a committed group of stakeholders who are helping the work become a sustained success. Includes coalition with students and families, educators and school administrators, district staff, and others within and beyond the community.
    Culture
    Values, norms, and practices that support effective and sustainable innovation and learning for all. Includes a culture of trust and mutual respect, of collective practice, and of learning and innovation.
